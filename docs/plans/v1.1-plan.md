# Pandaro 1.1 plan: accounts, sync, rewards and polish

Status: **approved, decisions recorded in §9**, dated 26 October 2026.

## 0. The big change, stated up front

Pandaro 1.0 promised **no account, no server, no tracking**. Several items in this request
reverse that promise:

- user accounts and login;
- server backups;
- A/B testing, which needs usage data;
- email.

That is a legitimate product direction, but it changes Pandaro's privacy story, the store privacy
labels and the legal obligations.

**Recommendation:** keep Pandaro **local-first with optional accounts.** The app keeps working
fully without signing in. An account adds encrypted cloud backup and cross-device sync.
Experiments and analytics are **opt-in**. This keeps the strongest market gap (§1) while adding
what you asked for.

## 1. Market research

directional rather than as audited store data.

### Where the gap is

Users are not asking for _more features_. They are asking for an app that **doesn't betray them**:

1. **No ads and no bait-and-switch paywalls.** Pandaro is free, with no ads. If you ever charge,
   use a one-time purchase or cosmetic extras, and never take away features that were free.
2. **Never lose my data.** Sync and update data loss is the most damaging complaint across the
   category. Pandaro is already local-first; adding _optional_ encrypted backup and sync that is
   tested against conflicts and upgrades turns this into a headline feature.
3. **A timer that actually works in the background.** Several apps stop or mis-ring. Pandaro's
   timestamp engine already handles this, and the store copy should say so plainly.
4. **No forced sign-up.** Flora's forced social login is a direct complaint. Optional accounts
   keep the "works instantly" experience.
5. **Kind gamification.** Research on broken streaks (see §3) and the Finch feedback suggest that
   rewards which reward without punishing are an open niche. Most focus apps either have no
   rewards or have stressful streaks.

**Positioning line:** _"The calm focus timer that never nags, never loses your progress, and
never punishes a day off."_

## 3. Rewards experiment (A/B test)

### What the evidence says

- **Broken streaks reduce continuation**, and highlighting the break makes it worse (Silverman &
  Barasch, _Journal of Consumer Research_; confirm the exact citation before quoting it).
- **Duolingo** separated the daily _goal_ from the _streak_ and reported **+3.3% Day-14
  retention** and **+10.5%** learners on a streak (relative figures, Duolingo's own blog).
- Duolingo's "choose your streak goal" screen reportedly helped retention even though the choice
  wasn't used. That comes from a second-hand source and is unverified.
- Practitioner sources suggest capped or forgiving streaks burn people out less. This is opinion,
  not established research.

### Variants (each user sticks to one arm)

| Arm | Name                                   | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A   | **No daily rewards** (control)         | Today's 1.0 behaviour: the garden grows per completed session, with no day-based rewards.                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| B   | **Fixed daily reward**                 | The first completed focus session each day earns **1 bamboo shoot**, a cosmetic currency spent in the garden. Missing a day costs nothing.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| C   | **Scaled reward**                      | The daily reward grows with consecutive active days (1, 2, 3, then capped at 5 shoots). A missed day steps the multiplier **down by one level, not to zero**.                                                                                                                                                          |
| D   | **Commitment reward** (Duolingo-style) | Onboarding asks: _"How many days will you commit to: 3, 7, 14 or 30?"_ (no preselection). The reward scales with the commitment (3 → ×1, 7 → ×1.5, 14 → ×2, 30 → ×3), plus a completion bonus. **Two grace days** per commitment; missing a day _pauses_ rather than resets. Users can renew or change the commitment. |

### Guardrails, in every arm

- Rewards are **cosmetic only**: garden items and panda accessories. They never gate the timer,
  tasks or statistics.
- **No loss framing.** Never say "you lost your streak". Say "Welcome back. Your garden waited
  for you."
- A **"turn off rewards" switch** in Settings.
- Reward notifications are **off by default** and opt-in.

### Measurement

**Metrics:**

- **Primary:** Day-7 and Day-30 retention (opened the app and completed one or more sessions).
- **Secondary:** completed sessions per active week, and focus minutes.
- **Guardrails:**
  - share of interrupted or skipped sessions;
  - share of users turning rewards off or disabling notifications;
  - an optional one-question "How does Pandaro make you feel?" pulse at Day 14.

**Assignment:** a deterministic hash of a random install id picks the arm, with an even 25/25/25/25
split. Pure functions mean every arm is unit-testable.

**Data collection:**

- only from users who **opt in** to "Help improve Pandaro";
- events are aggregate-friendly, with no task text, no email, and only the random install id;
- sent to Pandaro's own endpoint, with no third-party analytics SDK.

**Sample size:** detecting a 3-point change in Day-7 retention from a base of about 25% (α 0.05,
power 0.8) needs roughly **3,300 opted-in users per arm**, about **13,000 total**. With fewer
users, run A versus D first: two arms need about 6,600 users.

**Honesty note:** I can build the variants, the assignment logic, the event pipeline and an
analysis script, and I can simulate them. **Real results only exist once real users have used the
app for 30 or more days.** No result should be reported before that.

## 4. Accounts, security and backend

### Account model

- **Optional accounts.** Sign up with email and password, with passkeys as an option.
  - Phone-number sign-up is **not recommended**: SMS costs, SIM-swap risk, and extra personal data.
  - If you want phone sign-up anyway, add it later with a provider such as Twilio Verify.
- **One account per email:** emails are normalised (trimmed, lower-cased) with a unique index.
- **Phone uniqueness** (if added): E.164 normalisation and a unique index.
- **One account per device:** see the caveat below.
- **Account deletion inside the app** is required by both Apple (5.1.1(v)) and Google Play. It
  includes a server data purge and an email confirmation.

**Caveat on "one account per device".** Apple restricts fingerprinting, and Google Play forbids
linking hardware IDs to personal data. The compliant tools are Apple **DeviceCheck**, which gives
2 bits per device and survives reinstall, and the Android **Play Integrity API**. They can limit
**account _creation_** per device. They cannot enforce a strict one-account rule on the web, and a
strict rule blocks families sharing a tablet. **Recommendation:** allow at most 2 new accounts per
device per 30 days on native, plus IP- and email-based rate limits on the web.

### Authentication

- **Password hashing:**
  - Argon2id (memory 64 MiB, 3 iterations, parallelism 1), with a per-user salt and a server-side
    pepper kept in a secrets manager;
  - minimum 10 characters;
  - checked against a breached-password list using the k-anonymity range API (a third-party call;
    optional, needs your approval).
- **Sessions that survive switching servers:**
  - short-lived (15-minute) signed access tokens (JWT, Ed25519) that **any server instance can
    verify** with no server memory;
  - rotating refresh tokens (30 days), stored **hashed** in the database, with reuse detection
    that revokes the token family;
  - if a request lands on a different server, or a server dies mid-process, the client
    transparently refreshes and retries, and the user stays logged in;
  - all write requests are **idempotent**, using a client request id, so a retry can't duplicate a
    session or task.
- **Rate limiting:** a sliding window per IP and per account, held in shared storage (Redis or
  Postgres) so it works across instances.
  - Login: 5 per 15 minutes per account and 20 per 15 minutes per IP, then exponential backoff.
  - Sign-up: 3 per hour per IP.
  - Password reset: 3 per hour per account.
- **Recovery:**
  - email-verified reset links: single use, valid 30 minutes, stored hashed;
  - a security email on password change or a login from a new device;
  - optional TOTP two-factor authentication.

### Data, encryption and backups

| Layer                     | Plan                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| In transit                | TLS 1.3 only, HSTS, certificate pinning not needed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| At rest                   | Managed Postgres with encrypted storage (AES-256); encrypted backups                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Task text (titles, notes) | **Option A (recommended for 1.1):** server-side field encryption with a key in a cloud KMS. **Option B:** end-to-end encryption with a key derived from the password. Stronger privacy, but a forgotten password means the synced data is lost unless the user saved a recovery key. |
| Backups                   | Point-in-time recovery (WAL, 7–14 days), daily snapshots copied to a second region, a **monthly restore drill** with the result recorded                                                                                                                                             |
| Availability              | 2 or more stateless API instances in different zones behind a load balancer; managed Postgres with a hot standby and automatic failover; rolling deploys with health checks, so no downtime during releases; status page                                                             |
| Sync                      | Local-first stays the source of truth on the device. Sync pushes and pulls changes per record (an append-only session log needs no conflicts; tasks use last-writer-wins with server timestamps and a per-field merge for `done`). Every sync is idempotent.                         |

**Target:** 99.9% monthly availability for sync and login. Offline use is unaffected by any
server outage.

### Suggested stack

- **Monorepo** (npm workspaces):
  - `apps/web` (the current app);
  - `apps/api` (Fastify, TypeScript, zod shared with the client);
  - `packages/shared` (domain logic and schemas).
- **Database:** Postgres, with Drizzle or Kysely and SQL migrations.
- **Hosting:** decision needed (see §9). Options are Fly.io or Render (simplest), or AWS (most
  control).
- **Email:** a transactional provider (Postmark or Amazon SES), with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up.

**What I can do here:** build and test the API, migrations, auth, rate limiting, sync and the
backup/restore scripts. I can test failover locally, using Docker Compose with two API instances,
a load balancer, Postgres primary and replica, and kill-a-node tests.

**What needs you:** a cloud account, a domain, an email provider account, KMS or secrets setup,
and the production deployment.

## 5. Legal, consent and settings

Documents (drafted by me; **a lawyer must review them before launch**):

- **Terms of Service:** acceptable use, account termination, limitation of liability, governing
  law (your jurisdiction is needed).
- **Privacy Policy** (rewritten), listing the data now collected:
  - email and hashed password;
  - synced tasks and sessions;
  - optional usage events;
  - IP addresses in security logs (30-day retention).

  It also covers legal bases (GDPR / UK GDPR), CCPA/CPRA rights, processors (host, email,
  database), international transfers, retention, and children (13+, 16+ in parts of the EU, or
  parental consent).

- **Data Processing and Retention Notice:** what is kept, where, for how long, and how to export
  or delete it.
- **Cookie and storage notice:** local storage only, with no tracking cookies.

**Consent and toggles.** Consent is versioned, timestamped and revocable. Data-sharing toggles
default to off, except where noted.

## 6. Animations

These use CSS and the Web Animations API, with no new library. They must not slow the app down,
and each is reduced by "Reduce animation" or `prefers-reduced-motion`.

- **View transitions:** a soft fade and slide between Timer, Tasks, Stats and Garden (the View
  Transitions API where supported).
- **Timer:**
  - a ring "completion bloom" when a session ends;
  - each session dot pops as it fills;
  - the play and pause icon morphs between states;
  - the digits roll on minute changes.
- **Panda:**
  - more idle behaviours (ear twitch, glancing around, yawning when paused for a long time);
  - a smooth cross-fade between poses;
  - a happy bounce when a task is completed.
- **Tasks:**
  - a checkmark stroke-draw;
  - completed tasks slide to the Done list;
  - drag-reorder settles smoothly;
  - an "undo" toast slides in.
- **Garden and rewards:**
  - bamboo grows segment by segment when you return after earning;
  - an unlock reveal for new decorations;
  - a reward "shoot" flies to the garden counter.
- **Stats:** bars rise and heat-map cells fade in on first view.
- **Budget:** a frame rate of 60 fps on a mid-range Android. Animations are paused when the page
  is hidden, and none loops faster than every few seconds during focus.
